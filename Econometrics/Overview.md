**TIPS FOR ECONOMETRICS** 

I think you basically just have to study everything since there's no choice. There are four questions in the exam (2 microeconometrics, 2 macroeconometrics). The first microeconometrics question will usually be standard OLS, asymptotics, testing or heteroskedasticity (or a mix). The second question will usually be some variation of maximum likelihood. The first macro econometrics question will usually be some sort of time series asymptotics (martingale difference sequences, or AR(1) processes). The second question will usually be some testing question or ECM/Co-integration. 

It is therefore important to actually go and memorise every single test in existence for macroeconometrics (even those like Perron's test or KPSS test) because they do actually test those stuff (as you'll come to know). 

To remember proofs and derivations, you should split the proofs into milestones. In other words, you have a mathematical object that you split into different terms, and you should know which each of them converge to and then work towards those convergences. This is to avoid remembering (which is impossible) every single thing.
