**TIPS FOR MICROECONOMIC ANALYSIS**

Don't want to fear monger but this paper is just tough. The lectures give you a false sense of confidence (because the lectures are not hard) but the exam is difficult. The exam is also choiceness (i.e. 4 questions) and the lecturer envisages to bundle every 2 weeks of topics into each question. I am going to split the tips based on the topics. My overall tip is to really look at past year papers (especially the past two years) to get a feel.

*Linear Algebra and Multivariate Calculus*
I said that the paper is tough. A lot of it is because of this part in the exam. But the good news is that a huge part of what is tough about this paper is simply that they test things that you don't really encounter (especially for linear algebra and multivariate calculus) within the course but are actually quite basic things (for instance really basic real analysis). As such, this can be circumvented by going to the Oxford Math Moodle page and pulling up the Analysis Notes and skimming though. The relevant notes to look through would be Analysis II (Continuity and Differentiability). Note: the content will not be 1-1 but they have tested things that are commonplace in Analysis II (such as Lipschitz continuity...who knows they might test uniform continuity or something). This is obviously if you want to feel safe in getting 75+. You could probably get a first class in the paper (as many of us did) without doing the above. 

*Constrained Optimisation and Information Economics*
By far the easiest section of this course. Study this and be sure to rack up all the marks you can here because it's not guaranteed to be easy anywhere else. (Though some Kuhn Tucker problems particularly in the early years can be tough). Be sure to know the Kuhn Tucker conditions through and through. Information Economics questions tend to be pretty standard as well and just a little harder than core microeconomics. 

*Decision Theory*
Split into two parts -- under certainty and uncertainty. The first part (under certainty) is akin to first year logic. Or at least, that's how the lectures make it feel. But in my experience with the past year papers, it can be really hard as well sometimes. As such, it is very important to get experience with the past year papers, more so for this section, than just reading the course notes. Under uncertainty is a bit more standard...I guess? Just make sure you really understand the definitions and can apply them.

*Search and Dynamic Optimisation*
Dynamic Optimisation is not very hard but is tedious. Search is quite conceptually challenging and is often asked in novel ways that makes it particularly difficult. Not much to say other than look at the past year papers to get a feel of what they ask and instead of remembering "formulas" which the lectures make you believe is all you need, you should know how to solve each problem from first principles so that you can solve the novel problems given.
