# Oxford-PPE-FHS
I took the following courses -- here are some of my thoughts 
| Subject  | Thoughts |
|----------|----------|
| Core Microeconomics   | Honestly its not that easy -- there is a lot of content to be learnt, and additional mathematical knowledge is pretty useful. In particular, learning and understanding the signalling models/Stiglitz-Rothschild models is quite hard |
| Ethics   |  I hated this course because I don't like the ``methodology" of ethics, which is basically thought experiments. Overall, I found it pretty hard but I think its the easiest philosophy paper      |
| Knowledge and Reality   | Most of the real and big topics, like knowledge, modality etc are pretty hard. I recommend doing more applied, narrow topics like contextualism, epistemic injustice and metaphysics of race (if taught). These tend to be significantly easier.   |
| Theory of Politics | Very easy |
| Quantitative Economics    |  Much harder than I expected, because a lot of the theoretical results are waved away without a thorough proof/explanation. Like Core Micro, a lot to know without a common thread, and taking the time to work through the derivations are useful. |
| Philosophical Logic    | Its reputation is that its super difficult -- and I agree for the most part. I don't think the content itself is particularly difficult (except maybe Second Order Logic and being familiar with the Lowenheim-Skolem Theorem) but the exam is a very high variance paper and can be extremely difficult.   |
| Econometrics    | I also don't think this paper is as hard as people make it out to be in terms of the content. I believe the exams are another story. I do think the second half (time series) of the course is much harder than the first half, in part because it is much less coherent and you're just learning a hodgepodge of things without a common thread.   |
