# Oxford-PPE-FHS
This is my repository of notes for the Oxford PPE papers I took. I also included some thoughts on each paper. 

**How to Use**
DO NOT USE for consolidation. My notes are more on the comprehensive end and serve to plug any knowledge gaps and to explicate most results (say, in economics or logic) more fully. You should probably create your own summaries. 

| Subject  | Thoughts |
|----------|----------|
| Core Microeconomics   | Honestly its not that easy -- there is a lot of content to be learnt, and mathematical knowledge which you technically are taught is pretty useful (like the Kuhn-Tucker conditions for inequality constraints, in the context of public good optimisation problems). In particular, learning and understanding the signalling models/Stiglitz-Rothschild models is quite hard |
| Ethics   |  I hated this course because I don't like the "methodology" of ethics, which is basically treating intuitions and thought experiments as the "data" of our inquiry. I very much preferred the metaethics aspect of the course, especially moral realism. Overall, I found it pretty hard but I think its the easiest philosophy paper (other than theory of politics)  |
| Knowledge and Reality   | Most of the real and big topics, like knowledge, modality etc are pretty hard. I recommend doing more applied, narrow topics like contextualism, epistemic injustice and metaphysics of race (if taught). These tend to be significantly easier. Note that not all the topics come out, but some reliably come out.|
| Theory of Politics | Very easy. A lot of topics are similar to year 1 theory of politics. No more author/text based questions. Be judicious in topic selection because some topics alternate with a "twin" topic in the exam (justice & injustice, political obligation & civil disobedience) |
| Quantitative Economics    |  Much harder than I expected, because a lot of the theoretical results are waved away without a thorough proof/explanation. Like Core Micro, a lot to know without a common thread, and taking the time to work through the derivations are useful. At the same time, I think it's not clear what the "correct" answers are at times -- prioritise doing the mathematical questions because those have closed answers. While you can get by with doing minimal time series, I think being good at time series is high ROI because the question is always doable in the exam. |
| Philosophical Logic    | Its reputation is that it's super difficult -- and I agree for the most part. I don't think the content itself is particularly difficult (except maybe sometimes being familiar with metalogical results such as compactness of the Lowenheim Skolem theorem) but the exam can be a very high variance paper and can sometimes be extremely difficult. The essays are also pretty hard -- I'd recommend being as simple as possible, sticking with what you're confident in and satisficing on the essays. However, the past few exams (2024-2026) have been quite easy. |
| Econometrics    | I also don't think this paper is as hard as people make it out to be in terms of the content. The exams have become easier in the recent years and just require you to have a good grasp of the fundamentals and not be careless. I do think the second half (time series) of the course is much harder than the first half, in part because it is much less coherent and you're just learning a hodgepodge of things without a common thread.   |
| Microeconomic Analysis   |    Difficulty has spiked immensely for 2025-2026 papers. The exam treatment of linear algebra is much more proof based and multivariate calculus is a lot more like real analysis. I do think you have to be quite good at pure mathematics (not in the high school computational sense) but in abstract thinking and writing proofs. The main difficulty of this paper is the sheer gap between what is tested and what is taught. I would say this is by far the hardest paper I took. |
| Game Theory   |   I found game theory quite difficult. A lot of the questions were either quite tedious or conceptually difficult. Really depends on the exam paper and the topics that you're comfortable with coming out (you have to do four questions and I really only could comfortably do 3 in the exam). You have to choose the topics you focus on wisely for Part B (the common ones are Evolution, Repeated Games and Bargaining). But it is interesting and a lot of people who "like" economics will find game theory appealing. |
